# GoldKing — Project Context

## Purpose of This Document

This file defines GoldKing’s current **narrative and world canon**, including the protagonist, core premise, tone, themes, story guardrails, and major narrative questions.

Treat the information under **Established Canon** as the current narrative foundation of the project. Do not contradict or overwrite it without Quon or Jason explicitly changing it.

Gameplay systems, presentation, level structure, and production direction should be documented in the current gameplay/design foundation documents rather than duplicated here.

Ideas listed under **Open Questions** are not finalized canon.

**Last Reviewed:** 2026-11-11

---

## Project Overview

**GoldKing** is a game being developed by brothers Quon and Jason.

The story follows a formerly powerful king whose growing obsession with gold and riches caused him to neglect the people and responsibilities of his kingdom. He was not an evil or intentionally cruel ruler. He is fundamentally a good-hearted and likable person, but his greed became a serious flaw that distorted his priorities.

Eventually, the people of the kingdom had enough. They overthrew him, removed him from power, and cast him out.

The game begins at the moment of his fall.

---

## Core Premise

The player takes the role of the Gold King after he has been overthrown.

The opening begins with the king being thrown out of his own kingdom by an angry mob and landing on the outskirts with nothing. He has lost his throne, status, wealth, authority, and access to the kingdom he once ruled.

From there, the player must rebuild from nothing.

The Gold King’s main goal is to regain his kingdom.

How he earns it back, what he becomes along the way, and whether he truly deserves to rule again are central parts of the story.

# Established Canon

## The Gold King

The protagonist is known as the **Gold King**.

He is:

- Fundamentally good-hearted
- Naturally likable
- Charismatic and entertaining
- Flawed rather than evil
- Deeply attracted to gold, riches, treasure, and material wealth
- Capable of caring about others, even when his greed clouds his judgment
- Someone the player should enjoy following despite his mistakes

He should not be portrayed as cruel, malicious, heartless, or villainous by nature.

His defining flaw is greed.

His love of gold gradually became an obsession. Over time, gaining and possessing more wealth became increasingly important to him. This caused him to become distracted from his duties and neglectful of the kingdom.

His downfall came from misplaced priorities, not from a desire to hurt people.

## His Greed

The Gold King’s greed should be treated as a meaningful character flaw, not proof that he is secretly evil.

He may genuinely believe that more gold will provide safety, strength, happiness, influence, or the ability to fix future problems. However, his idea of “enough” keeps growing.

A useful way to understand his mindset is:

> Once I have enough gold, I can fix everything.

The problem is that he never truly reaches “enough.”

He understands the financial value of treasure, but he often fails to recognize the greater value of trust, responsibility, loyalty, relationships, and time.

His character arc does not necessarily require him to stop loving gold. Gold is part of his identity and should remain fun. His deeper challenge is learning whether he can control his desire for wealth instead of allowing it to control him.

## The Kingdom’s Rebellion

The kingdom’s people were justified in removing the Gold King from power.

They did not overthrow him because he was a sadistic tyrant. They overthrew him because his obsession with accumulating gold caused him to neglect the kingdom and the people who depended on him.

The exact events that finally triggered the rebellion have not yet been finalized.

## The Beginning of the Game

The story starts after the king has already lost.

The player sees the Gold King being physically cast out of the kingdom by an angry mob. He lands outside the kingdom, humiliated and stripped of everything that once defined his position.

He begins the game with nothing.

This opening should establish:

- His fall from power
- The anger of the kingdom
- His humiliation
- His personality
- The seriousness of his failure
- The possibility of humor within the situation
- His desire to return

## Primary Goal

The Gold King wants to reclaim his kingdom.

This provides the player’s main long-term objective.

The story should explore the difference between:

- Regaining possession of the kingdom
- Regaining the loyalty of its people
- Becoming powerful again
- Becoming worthy of ruling again

Reaching the throne and deserving the throne should not automatically be treated as the same achievement.

## Major Inspirations

GoldKing is heavily influenced by:

- Folklore
- Mythology
- Legends about treasure, greed, curses, kings, monsters, and magical bargains
- The story and symbolism of King Midas

The game may reinterpret myths and folklore rather than directly retelling them.

King Midas is a major thematic influence, especially the idea that the thing a person desires most can become destructive when pursued without restraint.

---

## Tone

The tone should balance:

- Adventure
- Myth
- Humor
- Character-driven storytelling
- Consequences
- Emotional growth
- Wonder
- Occasional tragedy or darkness

The Gold King’s downfall can be serious without making the entire game grim.

He should be capable of dramatic, funny, proud, ridiculous, sincere, and emotionally vulnerable moments.

The game should allow the player to laugh at the Gold King while still caring about him.

---

## Central Themes

Current themes include:

### Greed and Desire

Wanting wealth is not automatically evil, but unchecked desire can distort a person’s values.

### Value Versus Price

The Gold King is skilled at recognizing what things cost, but not always what they are truly worth.

### Power and Responsibility

Being able to reclaim a throne does not prove that someone deserves to rule.

### Loss and Identity

After losing his gold, crown, kingdom, and title, the Gold King must discover who he is without them.

### Redemption

The Gold King may have the opportunity to grow, repair damage, and earn back trust. Redemption should involve actions and sacrifice rather than simply defeating an enemy.

### “Enough”

The story should repeatedly challenge the idea that one more treasure, one more victory, or one more pile of gold will finally satisfy him.

---

## Creative Guardrails

Future ideas should generally follow these rules:

1. **Do not turn the Gold King into an evil tyrant.**  
   His actions may cause harm, but cruelty is not his true nature.

2. **Do not remove his greed completely.**  
   His love of gold is one of the game’s defining traits and should remain part of his charm, humor, conflict, and identity.

3. **Do not make the kingdom’s citizens unreasonable villains simply for overthrowing him.**  
   Their anger should come from genuine neglect and suffering.

4. **Do not make reclaiming the throne automatically equal redemption.**  
   The emotional and moral journey should matter as much as the external victory.

5. **Keep mythology and folklore central to the world.**  
   Creatures, regions, treasures, curses, characters, and quests should feel connected to mythic storytelling.

6. **Preserve the balance between humor and consequence.**  
   The king can be theatrical and funny without making the suffering caused by his neglect meaningless.

7. **Gold should be more than currency.**  
   It should function as a symbol, temptation, source of power, source of conflict, and reflection of the king’s internal struggle.

## Useful Story Question

A central question for the game is:

> Will the Gold King earn his kingdom back, buy it back, conquer it back, or become worthy of it for the first time?

This question is thematic guidance, not a finalized ending.

- Who rules the kingdom after the Gold King is removed
- What specific event caused the final uprising
- Whether the rebellion was spontaneous or organized
- Whether someone manipulated the people or took advantage of the king’s failures
- How the Gold King originally became ruler
- Whether his obsession has a magical origin
- Whether gold provides supernatural abilities
- Whether there is a literal Midas-like curse
- How companions react to his greed
- Whether the player can make meaningful moral choices

These subjects should be treated as areas for exploration rather than established facts.

## One-Paragraph Summary

GoldKing is a folklore- and mythology-inspired game about a good-hearted, charismatic king whose growing obsession with gold caused him to neglect his kingdom. His people eventually overthrew him and cast him out. The game begins with the disgraced Gold King landing outside his former kingdom with nothing. The player must rebuild from the ground up and work toward reclaiming the throne. Although the king is likable and not evil, his greed remains his defining flaw. The story explores wealth, value, responsibility, identity, redemption, and whether regaining a kingdom is the same as becoming worthy of ruling it.
